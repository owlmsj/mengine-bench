Piet Mondrian · 1930 · De Stijl

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A few lines, three colours, and a balance that reshaped modern design.

1930

The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), Dutch painter and co-founder of De Stijl.

The year

1930

Oil on canvas. Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Medium
Oil on canvas
Collection
Kunsthaus Zurich

The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Principles

Neoplasticism limits the vocabulary:

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines
  • Three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • Non-colours: white, grey and black

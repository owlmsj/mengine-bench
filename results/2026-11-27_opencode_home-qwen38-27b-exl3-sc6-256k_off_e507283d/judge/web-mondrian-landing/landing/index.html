The painter and the movement

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. This canvas belongs to De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Within De Stijl, Mondrian worked in the style he called Neoplasticism: a reduction of painting to its most basic elements, in search of a universal visual order.

2

The rules of Neoplasticism

The work obeys three strict principles.

Only horizontal & vertical lines

No diagonals, no curves. The composition is built entirely from right angles.

The three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow — nothing else, no secondary hues.

The non-colours

White, grey and black complete the palette.

3

The picture

A large red square sits at the top right. A small blue square sits at the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle sits at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground into these blocks.

4

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

A handful of flat planes, held in tension by black lines, proved that a painting could be both radically simple and structurally alive — an idea that outlasted the canvas and shaped the design of the twentieth century.

Asymmetric balance: one large block, two small, offset across the grid.

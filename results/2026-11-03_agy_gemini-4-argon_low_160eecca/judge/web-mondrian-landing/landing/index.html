Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow

Painted in 1930 by Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), this oil on canvas work in the Kunsthaus Zurich collection distills painting to the core principles of De Stijl and Neoplasticism: only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

The picture: a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944)
Year & Medium 1930, Oil on canvas
Collection Kunsthaus Zurich
Top right: a large red square anchored by thick black horizontal and vertical lines, counterbalanced diagonally by the small blue square at the bottom left and the small yellow rectangle at the bottom right.

De Stijl & the Principles of Neoplasticism

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its visual philosophy, Neoplasticism, restricts form and colour to strict geometric essentials.

Orthogonal Lines

Only horizontal and vertical lines divide the composition.

Primary Colours

The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow.

Non-Colours

The non-colours: white, grey and black.

Why the Work Matters

"Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture."

Rather than relying on symmetry, Mondrian placed the large red square at the top right and balanced it across thick black lines on a white ground with a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right.

The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. In 1917, together with Theo van Doesburg, he founded the De Stijl movement in the Netherlands.

His painting belongs to De Stijl's programme of neoplasticism: reduction of a picture to the fewest possible elements.

The work

  • Title Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow (also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow)
  • Painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944)
  • Year 1930
  • Medium Oil on canvas
  • Collection Kunsthaus Zurich

The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Its neoplasticism allows only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

De Stijl · Neoplasticism

Composition with
Red, Blue and Yellow

by Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter · 1930 · oil on canvas

1930 Kunsthaus Zurich

01The work

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right — divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. Oil on canvas, held in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

Red Blue Yellow White Grey Black

The composition, rebuilt to the fact sheet.

02The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, called Neoplasticism, restrict the picture to a strict grammar.

Only horizontal & vertical lines

No diagonals. The grid is the only structure.

The three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow — nothing else.

The non-colours

White, grey and black complete the field.

Asymmetric balance

A few blocks, weighted, never centred.

03Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The grammar Mondrian fixed here — primary colour, straight line, white ground — is still the vocabulary of a century of layout.

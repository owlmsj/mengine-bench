De Stijl · 1930

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A single grid of black lines, white ground and three primary colours — Piet Mondrian's most distilled statement of Neoplasticism, painted in 1930.

The painter

Piet Mondrian, 1872–1944

Dutch painter. He co-founded the De Stijl movement in the Netherlands in 1917, alongside Theo van Doesburg, and reduced painting to its purest structure.

The year

1930

Oil on canvas, now in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, called Neoplasticism, allow only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours — red, blue and yellow — and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Why it matters

An asymmetric balance that outlived the canvas

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground. That asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

The picture

Red, blue, yellow — and nothing else

Only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours, and the non-colours white, grey and black. No diagonal, no curve, no secondary hue.

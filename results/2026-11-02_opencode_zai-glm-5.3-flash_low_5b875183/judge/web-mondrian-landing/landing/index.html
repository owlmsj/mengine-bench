One painting · 1930 · De Stijl

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

By Piet Mondrian, oil on canvas, 1930. A large red square, a small blue square, a small yellow rectangle — thick black lines on white. Nothing more, and that is the point.

The facts

  • PainterPiet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter
  • Year1930
  • MediumOil on canvas
  • CollectionKunsthaus Zürich
  • MovementDe Stijl — founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian

Why it matters

The rules

Mondrian's principles, known as Neoplasticism, allowed only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours — red, blue and yellow — and the non-colours white, grey and black.

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

The influence

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

That is the whole story: a Dutch painter, the De Stijl movement, and one 1930 canvas whose balanced asymmetry still echoes in design and architecture today.

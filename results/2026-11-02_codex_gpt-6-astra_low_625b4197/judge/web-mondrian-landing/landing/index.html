01 / THE LANGUAGE

Less to look at.
More to see.

Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) worked with a pared-back visual language: horizontal and vertical lines, primary colours, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

De Stijl / Neoplasticism

A few elements. A careful balance.

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its Neoplasticist principles give this composition its vocabulary.

A large red square at the top right. A small blue square at the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground.

02 /
WHY IT MATTERS

A reference for the world beyond the canvas.

The asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

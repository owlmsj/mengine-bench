# Brazil's anti-inflation plans: editor's dossier

**Finding: Real leads, followed by Collor I and Collor II.** This is the ordering under the supplied rubric, not a finding that every podium plan fulfilled its promises. Real combines the strongest measured inflation reduction with the rubric's maximum durability credit. The Collor results reflect improvement from exceptionally high starting inflation, while leaving very high inflation afterward.

**Method and assumptions.** The IPCA figures are IBGE observations republished by the [Central Bank][A]. December's trailing-year rate equals the calendar-year rate. Use the year before and year after launch, exactly as [RUBRIC.md](./RUBRIC.md) requires; do not substitute the launch year's rate or annualize a monthly rate. Scores and month counts are our calculations, not statistics quoted from a source. †Each score links to its inflation inputs; its date inputs are [BCB's historical compendium][B], with weights and clamping prescribed by the local rubric. *Real's [60 months](./RUBRIC.md) are the sole imposed duration: their source is the supplied rubric, not a public observation.

Completed months mean the calendar year/month difference, subtracting a month when the successor's day of month precedes the launch day. Use only the rubric's designated successors, so Cruzado's clock runs to Bresser despite the intervening Cruzado revision. Dates use currency implementation for Cruzado and Collor I and the initial stabilization instrument for Bresser, Verão and Collor II. For Real, use the currency launch on [1994-07-01][B]; the [BCB also dates earlier implementation to February 1994][C]. That alternative would shorten Collor II's credited duration without changing the podium. The JSON retains the rubric's ASCII name “Verao.”

**Editorial interpretation.** Cruzado, Bresser and Verão all have higher after-period inflation than before-period inflation, so their inflation-reduction component is floored at zero; their positive scores come entirely from time until replacement. Collor I's after-period overlaps Collor II, so it cannot be read as an isolated effect of Collor I. Collor II's long gap to Real likewise does not establish sustained price stability. The [BCB's historical account][H] describes the pre-Real efforts as unsuccessful at defeating very high inflation.

**Defensible framing:** “Real wins the specified inflation-and-duration comparison; the Collor plans take the remaining podium places under that metric.” Avoid calling the podium a causal ranking or a complete promises audit: the rubric omits recession, blocked savings, distributional costs and the persistence of low inflation within each interval.

**Verification.** Every inflation input was matched to the public annual-equivalent series; dates were checked against the public BCB chronology. Scores use unrounded arithmetic, and the machine-readable file records source URLs, year windows and calculation provenance.

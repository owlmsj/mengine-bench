Piet Mondrian — Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow — 1930

Three colours, red, blue, yellow, and the black lines that hold them.

In 1930 the Dutch painter Piet Mondrian reduced the picture to its essentials: horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours, and the non-colours white, grey and black. The result is a balance of a few blocks that still shapes graphic design and architecture today.

The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter.

The year

1930

The medium

Oil on canvas

The collection

Kunsthaus Zurich

The movement

De Stijl — founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

The principles (Neoplasticism)

Only horizontal and vertical lines; the three primary colours and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

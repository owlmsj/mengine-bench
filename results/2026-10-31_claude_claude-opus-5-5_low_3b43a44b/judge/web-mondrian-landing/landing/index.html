Piet Mondrian, 1930

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A few blocks of primary colour, thick black lines and a white ground. This is how one oil painting became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

See how it is built

The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872-1944) was a Dutch painter. In 1917, in the Netherlands, he and Theo van Doesburg founded the movement this painting belongs to.

1930

Oil on canvas, in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich. Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow.

De Stijl

De Stijl began with a strict set of rules. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, reduce a painting to line and a handful of colours. Everything else is left out.

Only two directions

Lines run horizontally or vertically, and no other way.

Three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow are the only colours allowed.

  • Red
  • Blue
  • Yellow

Three non-colours

White, grey and black complete the palette.

  • White
  • Grey
  • Black

The picture

A large red square sits at the top right. A small blue square answers it at the bottom left, and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right.

Thick black lines divide them on a white ground.

Why it matters

The balance is asymmetric: one large block is held in place by two small ones. That arrangement of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

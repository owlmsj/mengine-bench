02 // The Year

1930

Painted in 1930 in Paris, marking the pinnacle of Mondrian's classic Neoplastic compositions in oil on canvas.

03 // The Painter

Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), Dutch Painter

Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), Dutch painter, transformed modern art by stripping away figurative narrative in pursuit of universal plastic relations. Moving from early Dutch landscapes through analytical Cubism, he arrived at a rigorous visual grammar of perpendicular black lines and primary planes.

Preserved in the Kunsthaus Zurich collection, his 1930 Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow exemplifies this lifelong quest for dynamic equilibrium.

Collection

Kunsthaus Zurich

Medium: oil on canvas. Housed in the permanent collection of Kunsthaus Zurich in Switzerland.

04 // The Movement

De Stijl (Netherlands, 1917)

De Stijl, founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, sought an objective artistic language uniting painting, architecture, and typography through pure geometric abstraction.

Neoplasticism // 01

Orthogonal Lines

Built strictly from only horizontal and vertical lines, rejecting curves and diagonals to establish structural clarity.

Neoplasticism // 02

Primary Colours

Restricted to the three primary colours red, blue and yellow in flat, unmodulated fields.

Neoplasticism // 03

Non-Colours

Counterbalanced by the non-colours white, grey and black for ground planes and dividing lines.

05 // Why the Work Matters

A Blueprint for Modern Design

Why it matters: its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Modern Graphic Design

Asymmetric Grid Systems

Designers adopted Mondrian's unequal rectangular divisions and bold structural rules to organize editorial pages, Swiss posters, and responsive web layouts without relying on static bilateral symmetry.

Modern Architecture

Structural Planes in Space

Architectural modernism translated the painting's horizontal and vertical lines and primary-colour blocks into curtain walls, modular facades, and open spatial volumes.

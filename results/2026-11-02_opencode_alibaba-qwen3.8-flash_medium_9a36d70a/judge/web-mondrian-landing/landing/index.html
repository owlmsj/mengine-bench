De Stijl · 1930

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter — oil on canvas. Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. Collection: Kunsthaus Zurich.

One large red square, one small blue square, one small yellow rectangle: a few blocks of primary colour, held apart by thick black lines on a white ground — and held together by an asymmetric balance that went on to influence modern graphic design and architecture.

Painter Piet Mondrian Year 1930 Movement De Stijl Medium Oil on canvas

The painter and the movement

The work was made in 1930 by Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), the Dutch painter who, together with Theo van Doesburg, founded the De Stijl movement in the Netherlands in 1917.

Painted in oil on canvas, the picture belongs to the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

Neoplasticism, in three rules

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines
  • Only the primary colours: red, blue, yellow
  • Only the non-colours: white, grey, black

The picture

A large red square occupies the top right. A small blue square sits at the bottom left; a small yellow rectangle closes the corner at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the fields on a white ground — nothing curved, nothing representational.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The vocabulary you see on this page — the grid, the rules, the three colours — is that legacy, used to tell the painting's own story.

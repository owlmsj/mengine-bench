01The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter and one of the founders of De Stijl, the movement the magazine of the same name spread across the Netherlands and Europe from 1917 onward. Together with Theo van Doesburg, he turned a small Dutch avant-garde publication into one of the most influential art theories of the twentieth century.

By 1930, when he painted Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow, Mondrian had already stripped his art down to its essentials: no curves, no representation, no accidents — only structure, colour and proportion, held in a deliberately asymmetric balance.

02The movement and its principles

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its painting theory, known as Neoplasticism, set strict rules for a universal visual language:

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines — no diagonals, no curves.
  • Only the three primary colours: red, blue and yellow.
  • Plus the non-colours: white, grey and black.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow is the movement’s manifesto made paint: thick black lines cross at right angles on a white ground, and the primaries appear as pure, flat blocks.

03Why the work matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. One canvas, three colours and a grid: a visual grammar that still shapes the way the designed world looks.

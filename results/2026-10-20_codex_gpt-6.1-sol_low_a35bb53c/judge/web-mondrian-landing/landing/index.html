De Stijl
The art of simplicity

The movement & its principles

Less becomes a language.

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl sought a visual language reduced to essentials. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, gave lines and colours a radical clarity.

01 / Direction

Horizontal. Vertical.

Only horizontal and vertical lines structure the composition.

Three primary colours.

Red, blue and yellow bring colour to the grid.

03 / Contrast

Space to breathe.

White, grey and black form the movement’s palette of non-colours.

02 / Why it matters

A small palette.
A lasting influence.

The large red square at the top right meets a small blue square at the bottom left and a yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground. This asymmetric balance of just a few colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

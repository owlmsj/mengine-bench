Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian · 1930

A few blocks, an asymmetric balance

Painted in 1930 by the Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow — also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow — is an oil on canvas now held by the Kunsthaus Zurich.

De Stijl Neoplasticism 1930 Kunsthaus Zurich

The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow is one of his works: an oil on canvas from 1930, today in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

The work belongs to De Stijl, founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles of Neoplasticism use only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours — red, blue and yellow — and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Why it matters

The picture is a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground. Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

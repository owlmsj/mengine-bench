Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

By Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter — 1930

Oil on canvas De Stijl Kunsthaus Zurich

The composition, rebuilt in CSS: red, blue, yellow, black lines, white ground.

The painting

A large red square sits at the top right, a small blue square anchors the bottom left, and a small yellow rectangle fills the bottom right — divided by thick black lines on a white ground. Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow, the canvas is reduced to the bare elements Mondrian called Neoplasticism: nothing that names a thing, only relations of line and colour.

De Stijl & Neoplasticism

Mondrian made this work as a standard-bearer of De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its program — Neoplasticism — allows only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Lines

Only horizontal and vertical. No diagonals, no curves — the picture is built from right angles alone.

Colours

Only the three primaries: red, blue and yellow, set against the non-colours white, grey and black.

Founded

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. A painting about nothing but relations — line, plane, colour — ended up teaching designers how to compose pages, signs and buildings.

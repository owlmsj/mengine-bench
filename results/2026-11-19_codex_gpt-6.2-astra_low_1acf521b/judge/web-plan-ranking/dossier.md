# Which Brazilian inflation plan delivered?

**Real ranks first, followed by Collor I and Collor II.** Real combined the steepest proportional fall in annual inflation with full durability credit. Collor I beat its longer-lived successor because inflation fell further from its starting level. Cruzado, Bresser and Verão earned all their points from time elapsed. **Reading the scores.** The [rubric][rules] assigns [70%][rules] to the proportional drop in IPCA, floored at zero and capped at one, and [30%][rules] to completed months as a share of [60][rules]. Each row links the inflation inputs, launch and successor date used in its calculation. Real receives the prescribed [60 months][rules]. The comparison uses the calendar years immediately before and after launch, omitting the launch year.

**Dating choices.** Bresser starts with the minister’s [launch address][bresser], Verão with the [original provisional measure][verao], and Real when the [currency entered circulation][real]. Durability counts monthly anniversaries to the next plan named in the rubric. It measures time to replacement, even if inflation had already revived.

**Editorial caution.** Collor I’s after-year includes the launch of Collor II. Its runner-up score describes a fall across that window, but the comparison cannot establish how much either plan caused. Even Real’s winning after-year still recorded [22.41% inflation][ipca].

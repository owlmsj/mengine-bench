Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A painting by the Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), made in 1930 in oil on canvas. A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

De Stijl & Neoplasticism

The work belongs to De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, reduce painting to three things.

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines
  • The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • The non-colours: white, grey and black

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

What you see

Nothing but rectangles: one large red, one small blue, one small yellow, held apart by thick black lines on white.

Where it lives

The oil on canvas, painted in 1930, is in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

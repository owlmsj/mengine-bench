Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian · 1930
De Stijl · Oil on canvas
Painter

Piet Mondrian

1872–1944. Dutch painter, co-founder of De Stijl.

The Work

1930 · Oil on Canvas

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. Held in the collection of Kunsthaus Zurich.

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Movement

De Stijl

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Its principles, called Neoplasticism, allow only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Principle

Neoplasticism

Only horizontal and vertical lines. Primary colours: red, blue, yellow. Non-colours: white, grey, black.

Why It Matters

A Reference for Modern Design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

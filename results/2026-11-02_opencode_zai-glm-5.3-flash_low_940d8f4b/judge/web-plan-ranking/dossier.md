# Dossier: Which Brazilian anti-inflation plan actually delivered?

**For the editor.** Six stabilization plans, 1986-1994, scored with the rubric in `RUBRIC.md`: 70% weight on the drop in annual IPCA (year before vs. year after launch) and 30% on how long the plan lasted until the next one. Every number below comes from a cited URL.

## Sources and method

- **IPCA (annual, calendar year):** IBGE SIDRA table 1737, variable 2265 ("IPCA - Variação acumulada em 12 meses"), December value of each year, which equals the IPCA for that calendar year. Retrieved directly from https://apisidra.ibge.gov.br/values/t/1737/p/198501-199612/v/2265/n1/1
- **Launch dates:** the plan's own Wikipedia entry, cross-checked against the standard chronology: Plano Cruzado (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Cruzado), Plano Bresser (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Bresser), Plano Verão (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Ver%C3%A3o), Plano Collor (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Collor), Plano Real (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Real).
- **Assumptions stated:** "annual IPCA" taken as the December 12-month accumulated IPCA from IBGE SIDRA (the IBGE's own series; a few secondary sites quote slightly different legacy figures for 1986-87 and 1991, e.g. 59.9%/415.8% and 480.2%, because of weight-revision conventions - we use the IBGE series consistently, one source, one methodology). "Whole calendar months" counted from launch date to the next plan's launch date, truncating partial months. Collor II dated 31 Jan 1991 (announced then, effective 1 Feb); Verão dated 15 Jan 1989. Real uses the rubric's fixed 60 months.

## The story the ranking tells

**Real (1994) is the only plan that delivered what it promised.** Inflation collapsed from 2477.15% (1993) to 22.41% (1995) and never returned to hyperinflation - the plan is still in force nearly 32 years later, which is why the rubric caps its durability at 60 months (1.0). Sources: IPCA figures from the SIDRA URL above; launch and no-successor status from https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Real

**Collor I (1990) is the surprising number two.** Confiscating deposits was brutal, but it genuinely broke the inertial spike: IPCA fell from 1972.91% (1989) to 472.70% (1991). It scores high on the drop, low on durability - inflation rebounded within a year and Collor II had to be launched 10 months later. Sources: SIDRA URL above; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano_Collor

**Collor II (1991) is a middling case:** a partial drop (1620.97% to 1119.10%) and 41 months of survival simply because no other plan came before the Real.

**Cruzado, Bresser and Verão all score zero on the drop** - in each case the IPCA of year Y+1 was *higher* than year Y-1 (Cruzado: 242.23 to 363.41; Bresser: 79.66 to 980.21; Verão: 980.21 to 1620.97). These were price-freeze plans that bought a few months of relief and then delivered worse inflation than before. Sources: SIDRA URL above; plan dates from the Wikipedia entries linked above.

## Defensibility note

The ranking is insensitive to the two conventions we had to choose: (1) even using the legacy 1986/1987 figures (59.9%/415.8%) Cruzado and Bresser still score 0 on the drop; (2) moving Collor II's launch to 1 Feb 1991 changes its months from 41 to 41 and Collor I's from 10 to 10, leaving the ranking unchanged. The one genuinely contestable judgment - counting Collor I's confiscation as a "drop delivered" when inflation was still 472.70% a year later - is the rubric's arithmetic, not ours; the dossier text above flags it so the editor can present it with that caveat.

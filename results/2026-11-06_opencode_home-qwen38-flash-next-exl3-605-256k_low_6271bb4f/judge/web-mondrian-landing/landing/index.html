Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A large red square, a small blue square, a small yellow rectangle, divided by thick black lines on a white ground. Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

The painter

Piet Mondrian
1872–1944

A Dutch painter who, in 1917, co-founded the De Stijl movement together with Theo van Doesburg.

The year

1930

The year Mondrian painted Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow, also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow.

The collection

Kunsthaus Zurich

The oil-on-canvas work is held in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl & Neoplasticism

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its visual grammar, known as Neoplasticism, reduced painting to a few strict elements.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow puts that grammar to work: nothing in the picture steps outside it.

Lines
Only horizontal and vertical lines
Colours

The picture

Three blocks, thick black lines, white ground

A large red square sits at the top right. A small blue square anchors the bottom left, and a small yellow rectangle closes the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the fields on a white ground.

Nothing is centred and nothing is symmetrical, yet the few blocks hold each other in balance.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

In 1930, Piet Mondrian reduced a painting to its essence: a large red square, a small blue square, a small yellow rectangle, thick black lines on a white ground. Nothing more. It became one of the defining images of modern art.

The Painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter who moved step by step from landscapes to pure abstraction. Together with Theo van Doesburg he founded De Stijl, and he turned its ideas into paintings built only from lines and primary colours.

The Year

1930

Oil on canvas. Today the work belongs to the Kunsthaus Zurich collection.

The Movement

De Stijl (“The Style”) was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian: a movement aiming for a universal visual language of pure abstraction.

The Principles — Neoplasticism

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines — no diagonals, no curves.
  • Only the three primary colours: red, blue and yellow.
  • Only the non-colours: white, grey and black.

The Picture

A large red square fills the top right. A small blue square sits at the bottom left, a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground — an exact recipe, and a perfect balance.

Why It Matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture — you still see this grid in posters, interfaces and buildings today.

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

The work works by the principles of Neoplasticism: only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black. Nothing else enters the picture.

The movement

The painting belongs to De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Lines

Only horizontal and vertical lines.

Colours

The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow.

Non-colours

White, grey and black.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

A little red, a little blue, a sliver of yellow — and everything else white. The weight of each block is set against the empty ground, not against other blocks of equal size.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow

Painted in 1930 by Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), this oil on canvas distills painting to horizontal and vertical black lines, three primary colours, and a white ground. Held at Kunsthaus Zurich, it stands as a landmark of De Stijl and Neoplasticism.

De Stijl and Neoplasticism

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Through the principles of Neoplasticism, Mondrian reduced visual form to its barest elements.

1917

Founded in the Netherlands by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Only horizontal and vertical lines

Neoplasticism uses only horizontal and vertical lines, relying on right-angled black divisions to organize the plane.

Three primary colours

Only red, blue, and yellow appear as colour fields in the Neoplasticist vocabulary.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Without symmetry, ornament, or any element beyond primary colours, non-colours, and right-angled lines, the painting showed how proportion and placement alone can hold a whole composition in tension.

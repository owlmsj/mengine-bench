Piet Mondrian · 1930 · De Stijl

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

One canvas. Three primary colours, black lines, white ground — and a balance that reshaped modern design. The story of the painting that made a grid famous.

The painter · the painting

Piet Mondrian reduced a whole world to a grid

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter who spent his career stripping art down to its essentials — until a composition could be nothing but straight lines and flat colour.

In 1930 he painted Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow (also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow) in oil on canvas. A large red square sits at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left, a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right — divided by thick black lines on a white ground. The canvas belongs to the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl: art as pure order

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its answer to a broken post-war world was radical simplicity: art should not depict things, it should express universal harmony through pure relation.

Only horizontal & vertical

No diagonals, no curves. Every line meets another at a right angle.

Three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow — nothing mixed, nothing between.

Non-colours

White, grey and black carry the structure and the silence between colours.

This program had a name: Neoplasticism — painting rebuilt from zero, so that anyone, anywhere, could read it.

Why it matters

A few blocks that became a blueprint

The painting's asymmetric balance — a few primary-colour blocks held in tension by black lines on white — became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Every time a layout trades symmetry for tension, uses a grid instead of ornament, or lets one red square do the work of a thousand details, it is speaking Mondrian's language. The canvas is quiet; its influence is everywhere.

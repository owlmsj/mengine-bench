The story Painter · Movement · Picture · Legacy

The painter

Piet Mondrian, 1872-1944

Piet Mondrian (1872-1944) was a Dutch painter and, together with Theo van Doesburg, a founder of the De Stijl movement. Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow, painted in 1930 in oil on canvas, is his search for balance taken to its limit: a picture built only from straight lines and flat planes of colour.

The movement

De Stijl, Netherlands, 1917

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its members agreed on a strict visual programme — Neoplasticism — that reduced art to lines and colours, and nothing else.

The principles

Neoplasticism allows only

  • Horizontal and vertical lines
  • The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • The non-colours: white, grey and black

The picture

What you see

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Oil on canvas · Kunsthaus Zurich

Why it matters

A reference for modern design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

No perspective, no ornament, no curves: just a few rectangles held in equilibrium by thick black lines. Every grid system, poster and interface that ever balanced a large colour block against white space stands in this tradition.

Fewer elements. More balance.

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

The work is an oil on canvas, painted in 1930, and belongs to the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its painting language — Neoplasticism — restricts art to the essentials.

Only horizontal and vertical lines. Only the three primary colours red, blue and yellow. And only the non-colours white, grey and black.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Straight lines. Primary colours. Nothing else — and that is exactly why it still speaks.

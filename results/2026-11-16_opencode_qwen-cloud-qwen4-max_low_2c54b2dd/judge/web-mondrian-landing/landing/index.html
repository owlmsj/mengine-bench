The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. In 1917 he founded the movement De Stijl in the Netherlands together with Theo van Doesburg.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow, painted in 1930, is also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. It is an oil on canvas, held in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, called Neoplasticism, reduce painting to a strict vocabulary:

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines
  • The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • The non-colours: white, grey and black

The picture

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground. The composition you scrolled through above is built from exactly these elements.

Why it matters

An asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks.

That balance became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The page you are reading is set in the same language: horizontal and vertical lines, primary colours, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

How it works

Neoplasticism in a few strokes

Neoplasticism governs the image: only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

One large red square anchors the top right. A small blue square sits bottom left; a small yellow rectangle bottom right. Thick black lines divide them over a white ground — none of it symmetric.

  • Red — one large square, top right
  • Blue — one small square, bottom left
  • Yellow — one small rectangle, bottom right
  • Black — thick divider lines
  • White / grey — the ground between blocks
Why it matters

A reference for modern design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture — the painting that gave the language of straight lines and flat colours its canonical image.

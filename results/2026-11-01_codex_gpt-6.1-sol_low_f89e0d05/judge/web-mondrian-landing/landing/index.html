The movement

01

Less becomes
a language.

Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) helped found De Stijl in the Netherlands in 1917 with Theo van Doesburg. Its visual language sought simplicity through a precise vocabulary of lines and colours.

In Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow, also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow, that language takes shape as an asymmetric arrangement of coloured fields.

The principles / Neoplasticism

01Only horizontal and vertical lines.
02The primary colours: red, blue and yellow.
03The non-colours: white, grey and black.

Why it matters

02

A few colours.
A lasting influence.

The painting’s asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. A small visual vocabulary opened up an expansive way to compose.

# Brazil’s anti-inflation plans

**Editor’s finding.** Real ranks first, followed by Collor I and Collor II. Real combined the largest proportional fall in annual inflation with the rubric’s maximum durability credit. Collor I placed second on a larger measured inflation drop than Collor II. Its post-launch calendar year also includes Collor II, so the fall cannot be attributed to Collor I alone. Collor II’s score benefits from the long gap to Real, even though its post-launch annual inflation stayed above four digits. **How to read the scores.** The IPCA columns use IBGE’s national calendar-year rates for the year before and the year after launch. The score weights the proportional decline at 70% and durability at 30%. Rising inflation earns zero decline credit. Durability is elapsed whole months to the next launch listed in the rubric, capped at 60 months, with 60 assigned to Real. A month counts once the launch-day anniversary is reached, using month-end if that day does not exist. Scores are displayed to four decimals and ordered by the underlying calculation. [IBGE annual IPCA](https://www.ibge.gov.br/biblioteca/visualizacao/periodicos/236/inpc_ipca_2012_jan.pdf) · [Scoring rubric](./RUBRIC.md)

**Launch convention.** Dates mark the public rollout. For Verão, use the date of the announcement and signed measure. For Collor I, use the public presentation documented in contemporary research. For the phased Real plan, use the day the currency entered circulation. The table measures the gap to the next listed plan and compares annual outcomes. It cannot establish how long controls worked, isolate each plan’s effect, or assess costs such as the blocking of financial assets under Collor I.

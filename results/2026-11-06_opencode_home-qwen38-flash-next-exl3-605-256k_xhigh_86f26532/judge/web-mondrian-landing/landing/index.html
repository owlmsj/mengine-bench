Painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter.

Year

1930. Oil on canvas, now in the Kunsthaus Zurich.

Movement

De Stijl, founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

What the picture is

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Nothing in the picture repeats or mirrors the rest: the blocks differ in size and sit off-centre, and that asymmetric balance is what the painting is built from.

Neoplasticism

The principles behind the work allow only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

De Stijl

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow belongs to that movement, and it puts its rules on the canvas: straight lines, primary colour, white ground.

Why it still matters

Asymmetric balance: a few blocks of primary colour, unequal in size, held in equilibrium by thick black lines.

That balance became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

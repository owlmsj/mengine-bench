## Top 3, defensible
1. **Real (1994)** - the only plan that killed inflation: annual IPCA fell from 2477.15% (1993) to 22.41% (1995), a 99% drop, and it never had a successor plan. Score 0.9937.
2. **Collor I (1990)** - a real one-year drop (1764.98% in 1989 to 472.26% in 1991, -73%), bought partly by the savings confiscation, but it collapsed after 10 months when inflation returned. Score 0.5627.
3. **Collor II (1991)** - modest drop (1589.83% to 1119.14%, -30%) but the second most durable of the failed plans (41 months), carrying the economy to the Real. Score 0.4122.

The three freeze-first plans (Cruzado, Bresser, Verao) score near zero: in every case the calendar year after the launch was worse than the year before (Cruzado's 1987, 236.62%, even exceeded 1985's 226.22%), so the rubric's drop term is 0 and only durability counts.

## Assumptions
- Launch dates: Cruzado 28-Feb-1986 (DL 2.283 of 27-Feb-1986 took effect the 28th); Bresser 12-Jun-1987; Verao 15-Jan-1989 (MP 32); Collor I 16-Mar-1990 (MP 168; some sources say 15-Mar, no effect on month count); Collor II 31-Jan-1991; Real 01-Jul-1994 (currency day, Lei 8.880).
- months_lasted = whole calendar months between launch dates (floor); Real = 60 per rubric.
- ipca_before/after are calendar-year IPCA of Y-1 and Y+1, per rubric, not plan-anniversary windows.
- Cross-check note: an independent web-assistant lookup returned slightly different pre-1990 annual IPCA figures (e.g. 1985: 242.23, 1987: 363.41, 1989: 1972.91, 1990: 1620.97) but agreed on 1993 (2477.15) and 1995 (22.41); its plan-date URLs were not usable, so the table above keeps the standard IBGE series figures. The ranking and top 3 are identical under either set: the three freeze plans score drop=0 either way, and Real > Collor I > Collor II holds in both.

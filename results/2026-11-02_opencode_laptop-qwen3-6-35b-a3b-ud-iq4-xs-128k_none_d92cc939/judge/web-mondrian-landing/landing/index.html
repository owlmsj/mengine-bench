Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian · 1930

The Painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter who became one of the most influential figures of 20th-century abstract art. He co-founded the De Stijl movement, which sought pure abstraction through the simplest geometric forms.

Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow

Painted in 1930, this oil-on-canvas work is held in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich. The picture features a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

De Stijl & Neoplasticism

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Mondrian called his approach Neoplasticism, built on strict principles:

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines
  • Only the three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • And the non-colours: white, grey and black

Why It Matters

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow is celebrated for its asymmetric balance of just a few primary-colour blocks. This compositional clarity became a reference point for modern graphic design and architecture—its influence still echoes in everything from poster design to building facades.

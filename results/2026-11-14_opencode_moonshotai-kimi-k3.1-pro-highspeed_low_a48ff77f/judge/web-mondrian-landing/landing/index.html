Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian
Dutch painter, 1872–1944

A few blocks. Three primary colours. An asymmetric balance that changed the visual language of modern design.

A movement built for clarity

Mondrian made this work within De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian.

Only the essential

Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, permit only horizontal and vertical lines.

The palette is just as strict: the primary colours red, blue and yellow, with the non-colours white, grey and black.

Balance without symmetry

A large red square holds the top right. A small blue square anchors the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle sits at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

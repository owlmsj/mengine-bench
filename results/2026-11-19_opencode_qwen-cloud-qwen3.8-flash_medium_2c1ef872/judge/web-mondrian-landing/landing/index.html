Piet Mondrian · 1930 · Oil on canvas

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A large red square, a small blue one, a touch of yellow — held apart by thick black lines on white. One canvas that says everything De Stijl stood for.

01

De Stijl, Dutch in origin

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. In 1917 he and Theo van Doesburg founded De Stijl, the movement this 1930 oil on canvas belongs to.

The work is also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow, and today hangs in the Kunsthaus Zurich.

02

Neoplasticism: the whole vocabulary

Mondrian reduced painting to a strict set of elements. Only horizontal and vertical lines. Only the three primary colours. Only the non-colours.

On that vocabulary the picture builds its composition: a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left, a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right — divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

03

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. Grids, colour blocks and bold dividers you still see every day trace a line back to compositions like this one.

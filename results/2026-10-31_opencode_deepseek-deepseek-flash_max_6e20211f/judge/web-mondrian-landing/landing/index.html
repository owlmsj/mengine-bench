Piet Mondrian · 1872–1944 / De Stijl

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

A white ground, thick black lines and three blocks of primary colour. Painted in 1930, Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow is one of De Stijl's clearest statements: a picture with no subject beyond its own balance.

01 The painting

A white square, divided by weight.

Mondrian made the painting in 1930. It is oil on canvas, and it belongs to the Kunsthaus Zürich. The ground is white. Thick black lines cross it at right angles, dividing the surface into rectangles of different sizes.

Three fields carry colour. A large red square fills the upper right. A small blue square sits at the lower left. A small yellow rectangle closes the lower right. The rest is white, and the black lines hold the parts together.

The balance is asymmetric. The fields are unequal, and no side mirrors another. Yet the eye settles. That tension, rather than any scene or subject, is what the painting puts on view.

02 The painter

PietMondrian

Dutch painter · 1872–1944

Mondrian's mature work took the subject out of painting. What remained was line, colour and proportion.

Born in the Netherlands in 1872, he began with landscape and moved, step by step, toward abstraction. In 1917 he and Theo van Doesburg founded De Stijl, the movement that would give his work its rules.

Those rules were the ones named Neoplasticism: only horizontal and vertical lines, only the three primary colours, and white, grey and black. Mondrian worked inside them for the rest of his life. This painting, made in 1930, is one of the results.

03 The movement

De Stijl

1917The Netherlands

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl set out to reduce art to its elementary terms. Its principles are known as Neoplasticism.

    The line

    Only horizontal and vertical lines. No curves, no diagonals.

  2. 02

    The colour

    Red, blue and yellow. The primaries, used flat and unmixed.

  3. 03

    The ground

    White, grey and black. The non-colours that hold the space between.

  4. 04

    The balance

    Unequal rectangles, weighted against one another. Asymmetric balance is the painting's subject.

04 Why it matters

A reference for modern design.

The painting matters for what it leaves out. No scene, no figure, no shading: only line, field and proportion. A handful of rectangles, weighted against one another, hold each other in place.

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The lesson was the method: a grid, a limited palette and a proportion that keeps the eye moving.

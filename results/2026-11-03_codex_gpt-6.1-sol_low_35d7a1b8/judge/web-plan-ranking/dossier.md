# Brazil anti-inflation plans: editor dossier

**Finding:** Real leads, followed by Collor I and Collor II under the supplied rubric. This ranks outcomes; it does not establish which plan caused them.

**Method and assumptions.** Annual IPCA comes from the [IBGE historical table](https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/periodicos/236/inpc_ipca_2014_jul.pdf). Before and after are the calendar years immediately preceding and following launch. Durability counts completed months between the listed launches, subtracting a month if the ending day precedes the starting day. Use the [real currency introduction](https://www.bcb.gov.br/controleinflacao/planoreal?modalAberto=30anosreal_noticia4) as Real's launch, rather than its preparatory stages. The local RUBRIC.md supplies the weights, clamping and fixed Real horizon. Those assumptions have no public-source URL. All table scores and durations are calculations from linked inputs.

**Reading the ranking.** Real combines the strongest proportional inflation decline with maximum durability credit. Collor I has the next strongest decline but a short interval before its successor. Collor II gains from a smaller decline and the long interval to Real. Time until a successor is not evidence of sustained price stability. Cruzado, Bresser and Verao receive no inflation-drop credit because their specified after-year inflation exceeds the before-year figure; their positive scores come entirely from elapsed time.

**Editorial limits.** The after-year includes a successor for Cruzado, Verao and Collor I. This overlap prevents causal attribution. The score does not measure employment, output, fiscal costs, household losses or the precise promises made at launch. The [central bank history](https://www.bcb.gov.br/controleinflacao/planoreal?modalAberto=30anosreal_noticia4) treats Real as a staged process, so the chosen launch definition matters. Merely subtracting year-month labels would add a month to Cruzado and Collor II without changing the leading order.

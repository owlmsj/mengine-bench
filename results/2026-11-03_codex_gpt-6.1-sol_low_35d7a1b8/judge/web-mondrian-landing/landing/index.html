A few colours. A balance of opposites. An asymmetric composition that became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

1930Oil on canvas

A language of essentials.

Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) worked within De Stijl, the movement he and Theo van Doesburg founded in the Netherlands in 1917. Its principles of Neoplasticism use only horizontal and vertical lines, the primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Balance without symmetry.

A large red square holds the top right. A small blue square sits at the bottom left; a small yellow rectangle, at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground. This asymmetric balance of just a few primary-colour blocks made the work a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

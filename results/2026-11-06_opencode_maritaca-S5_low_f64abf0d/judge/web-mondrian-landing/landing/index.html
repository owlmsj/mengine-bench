Oil on canvas · 1930

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow.

Piet Mondrian reduced a painting to a white ground, thick black lines and three primary-coloured blocks — and made asymmetry into a discipline.

Red Blue Yellow White Grey Black
01

The painting

A large red square sits at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right — separated by thick black lines across a white ground. Painted in 1930 in oil on canvas, the work belongs to the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its theory of Neoplasticism set strict limits on what a picture could contain.

Only two directions

Horizontal and vertical lines — nothing diagonal, nothing curved.

Three primaries

Red, blue and yellow carry the colour; nothing else is allowed in.

The non-colours

White, grey and black form the ground, the line and the rest.

An unequal balance

The blocks differ in size and place; the balance comes from their arrangement, not from symmetry.

Red — primary Blue — primary Yellow — primary White — non-colour Grey — non-colour Black — the line
03

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture — a vocabulary of rectangles and primaries that designers still borrow from today.

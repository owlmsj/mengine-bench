01

The movement
The Netherlands, 1917

De Stijl / Neoplasticism

Less, with purpose.

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl reduced art to its essentials. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, offered a precise visual language of straight lines and elemental colour.

Two directions

Only horizontal and vertical lines. A structure built from right angles.

Three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow. Distinct blocks of colour, held in balance.

The non-colours

White, grey and black. Space and structure within the composition.

02 / Why it matters

A small palette.
A lasting
influence.

A large red square anchors the upper right. Small blue and yellow blocks punctuate the lower corners. Thick black lines divide a white ground: the balance is asymmetric, yet deliberate.

This balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The painting shows how much a simple visual language can express.

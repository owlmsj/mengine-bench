The painter

Piet Mondrian was a Dutch painter. In 1917, in the Netherlands, he and Theo van Doesburg founded De Stijl. Thirteen years later he painted this canvas, built from the movement's own short list of lines and colours.

1930, oil on canvas

The picture

Thick black lines divide a white ground. Colour appears in only three places, and each block differs in size. The weight sits high on the right, answered by two small notes along the bottom.

  • A large red square at the top right
  • A small blue square at the bottom left
  • A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. In the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The movement

De Stijl

Founded in the Netherlands by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, come down to three short rules.

1917
Rule 1

Only horizontal and vertical lines

Rule 2

The three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow.

Rule 3

The non-colours

White, grey and black.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

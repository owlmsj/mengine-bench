01 · The painter

Piet Mondrian

Piet Mondrian was a Dutch painter. In 1917, together with Theo van Doesburg, he founded De Stijl, the movement whose painting principles, known as Neoplasticism, reduced painting to horizontal and vertical lines, primary colours and non-colours. “Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow”, painted in 1930, follows those rules to the letter.

02 · The year

1930.

Oil on canvas, held at Kunsthaus Zurich: a large red square sits at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, all divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

De Stijl

04 · Why it matters

Asymmetric, yet in balance

A few primary-colour blocks, weighed against empty white and held by black lines: the painting’s asymmetric balance became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

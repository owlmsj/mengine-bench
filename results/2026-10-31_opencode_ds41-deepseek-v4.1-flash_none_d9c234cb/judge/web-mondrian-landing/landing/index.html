02 · The painter

Piet Mondrian, Dutch painter, 1872 to 1944.

The painter's dates are part of the story. Mondrian was born in 1872 and died in 1944, and this canvas belongs to the middle of that span: 1930, thirteen years after the founding of De Stijl.

In 1917 Mondrian and Theo van Doesburg founded De Stijl in the Netherlands. The movement's painting principles, gathered under the name Neoplasticism, gave Mondrian a fixed vocabulary: horizontal and vertical lines, the primaries red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black. This painting is that vocabulary at work, with nothing left over.

03 · The movement

De Stijl and the rules of Neoplasticism.

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Neoplasticism, the painting programme at its centre, was built on restriction: a short list of allowed elements, applied without deviation.

Horizontal and vertical lines

Only lines that stand or lie flat. The grid is not a subject here; it is the structure of the picture, holding every block in place.

Red, blue and yellow

The three primary colours carry the whole chromatic load of the work, laid down flat and unshaded.

White, grey and black

The non-colours complete the palette. White supplies the ground, black the edges, and grey the quiet interval between them.

05 · Why it matters

A few blocks, held in asymmetric balance.

Nothing in the painting is centred. The large red square pulls the weight to the top right, while the small blue and yellow blocks answer it from the lower corners. The black lines keep the parts apart and hold them together at the same time.

That balance, achieved with a handful of primary-colour blocks, is why the work reached beyond painting. Its asymmetric composition became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

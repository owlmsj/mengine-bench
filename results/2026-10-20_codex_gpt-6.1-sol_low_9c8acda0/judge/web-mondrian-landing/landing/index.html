Balance, rethought.

A large red square at the top right. A small blue square at the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right.

04 / The movement

Less form.
More possibility.

De Stijl · Netherlands · 1917

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl embraced a stripped-back visual language. Its principles of Neoplasticism made a few essential elements the basis of a composition.

Only two directions.

Horizontal and vertical lines. A structure built from right angles.

An essential palette.

The primary colours red, blue and yellow; the non-colours white, grey and black.

05 / Why it matters

A canvas that reached
beyond painting.

The asymmetric balance of just a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. This composition shows how much a limited visual vocabulary can express.

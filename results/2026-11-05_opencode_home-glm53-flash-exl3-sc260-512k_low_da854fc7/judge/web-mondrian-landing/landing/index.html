The painter

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944)

A Dutch painter who reduced painting to its bare essentials: only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black. This approach is known as Neoplasticism.

The movement

De Stijl

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles — Neoplasticism — strip art down to straight lines and primary colours.

LinesOnly horizontal and vertical

The picture

What you see

A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Why it matters

A reference for modern design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. One canvas from 1930 still shapes how grids, layouts and colour are used today.

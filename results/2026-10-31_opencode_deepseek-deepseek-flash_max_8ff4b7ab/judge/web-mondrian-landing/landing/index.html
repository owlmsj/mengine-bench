Neoplasticism · No. 02

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

In 1930, Piet Mondrian reduced painting to straight lines, three primary colours and a white ground. The result holds its balance without ever being symmetrical.

The principles of Neoplasticism

Principle 01 · Line

Only horizontals and verticals.

No diagonals, no curves. The straight line is the whole vocabulary.

Principle 02 · Colour

Red, blue and yellow. The primaries alone.

The three primary colours, unmixed. Every other colour is left out.

Principle 03 · Ground

White, grey and black.

The non-colours give the planes room to breathe, and the black lines their weight.

The record

A painting of very few things.

Painted in 1930, this canvas distils De Stijl into a handful of lines and planes. Here is what to know.

01 / The painter

Piet
Mondrian

Dutch painter · 1872–1944

A Dutch painter and co-founder of De Stijl. Mondrian made straight lines and primary colour the core of his work, and became one of the defining abstract artists of the twentieth century.

02 / The year

1930

Oil on canvas

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow. The painting is held in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zürich.

03 / The movement

De Stijl

The Netherlands · founded 1917

Founded by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl sought a universal visual language. Its principles of Neoplasticism are the grammar of this canvas: straight lines, primary colours and non-colours.

04 / The picture

One red, one blue, one yellow.

The composition

A large red square at the top right. A small blue square at the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the white ground between them.

05 / Why it matters

A few blocks. A new grammar.

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

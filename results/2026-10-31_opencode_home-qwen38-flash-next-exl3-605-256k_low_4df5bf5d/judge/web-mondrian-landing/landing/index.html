The painter and the painting

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter who spent his career stripping painting down to its essentials. In 1930 he completed Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow (also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow) in oil on canvas. The work belongs to the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

It is pure geometry: no figures, no landscape, no ornament — only planes of colour held apart by thick black lines.

De Stijl

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. The movement sought a universal visual language: pure abstraction, reduced to the most basic elements of art.

Mondrian called his version Neoplasticism. The painting of 1930 is its textbook statement — nothing in it exists that the theory did not allow.

The principles

  • Only horizontal and vertical lines — no diagonals, no curves
  • The three primary colours: red, blue and yellow
  • The non-colours: white, grey and black
  • Asymmetric balance — unequal parts held in equilibrium

What you see

A large red square fills the top right. A small blue square sits at the bottom left, and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Thick black lines divide the planes on a white ground. The heavy red mass and the small corner accents pull against each other and hold — tension without symmetry.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference point for modern graphic design and architecture. Grids, primary palettes and bold black rules — from posters and corporate identities to buildings — still echo this canvas. This very page is laid out along its lines.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian · 1930 · oil on canvas

The painter

Piet Mondrian
1872–1944

A Dutch painter, Mondrian is known for reducing painting to its most essential elements: straight lines and primary colour. This 1930 work belongs to his mature period of pure abstract composition.

The movement: De Stijl

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, called Neoplasticism, reduce art to a strict visual grammar:

Lines

Only horizontal and vertical lines. No diagonals, no curves.

Primary colours

Only the three primaries: red, blue and yellow.

Non-colours

And the non-colours: white, grey and black.

The picture

  • A large red square at the top right
  • A small blue square at the bottom left
  • A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right
  • Divided by thick black lines
  • On a white ground

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Three colour fields, a grid of black lines and a white ground: nothing added, nothing taken away. That restraint is what made the composition a lasting model for designers and architects.

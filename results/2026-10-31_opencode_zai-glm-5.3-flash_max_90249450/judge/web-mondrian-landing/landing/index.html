Piet Mondrian · De Stijl · 1930

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Oil on canvas · Kunsthaus Zürich · One of the most recognisable abstract paintings ever made.

The picture in one breath

In 1930, Piet Mondrian reduced painting to its bare essentials: thick black lines at right angles, a white ground, and three fields of pure primary colour — balanced so precisely that the empty space feels designed too.

01 · The painter

Piet Mondrian

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter who spent his career stripping art down to what he believed were its pure elements: line, plane and primary colour.

Together with Theo van Doesburg he founded De Stijl in the Netherlands in 1917. His radical simplification — no curves, no diagonals, no mixed colours — turned a canvas like this one into a manifesto you can hang on a wall.

03 · The movement

De Stijl

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl turned painting, design and architecture toward one radical idea: reduce everything to structure and pure colour. Its principles are known as Neoplasticism.

01

Two directions

Only horizontal and vertical lines. No diagonals, no curves — every edge meets at a right angle.

02

Three primaries

Only the primary colours are allowed.

03

The non-colours

Alongside the primaries: white, grey and black do the structural work.

04 · Why it matters

A reference for modern design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Nothing in the canvas is centred or symmetrical, yet everything is in equilibrium. That lesson — balance without symmetry — is why a painting from 1930 still looks at home next to a poster, a façade or a web layout today.

Graphic design

Grids, posters and layout systems still borrow its asymmetric structure and primary palette.

Architecture

Facade compositions and spatial planning echo its planes, lines and colour fields.

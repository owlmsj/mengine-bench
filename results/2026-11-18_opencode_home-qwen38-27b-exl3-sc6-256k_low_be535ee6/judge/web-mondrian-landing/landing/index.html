De Stijl · 1930 · Oil on canvas

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944), Dutch painter. A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Principles

Neoplasticism, as practised in De Stijl, allows only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

This picture follows those rules: primary-colour blocks set off by thick black lines on a white ground, arranged in asymmetric balance.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

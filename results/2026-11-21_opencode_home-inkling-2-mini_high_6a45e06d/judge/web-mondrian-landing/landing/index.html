01 / The painter

Piet Mondrian

Piet Mondrian (1872–1944) was a Dutch painter. He is the artist of Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow — also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow — painted in 1930 and held by the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The canvas is oil on canvas: a flat, hard-edged geometry that trades anything descriptive for the structure of the grid itself.

02 / The movement

De Stijl

De Stijl is the movement — founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its visual theory, Neoplasticism, is built on a small set of rules.

Lines

Only horizontal and vertical lines — never diagonals.

Primaries

Three colours: red, blue and yellow.

Non-colours

White, grey and black.

03 / The picture

A few blocks, held in tension

The picture is built from almost nothing, and that absence is the point. A large red square at the top right. A small blue square at the bottom left. A small yellow rectangle at the bottom right. Everything is divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

No figure, no depth, no narrative — just colour blocks balanced against open space.

04 / Why it matters

A reference for design

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

The work models how a flat grid can carry weight and meaning without a single conventional image — a method that is still readable in the logos, layouts and façades of the modern world.

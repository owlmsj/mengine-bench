The movement / De Stijl

01

Less on the canvas.
More in the balance.

Founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, De Stijl gave art a pared-back visual language. Its principles, known as Neoplasticism, reduced the picture to a precise vocabulary of lines and colours.

Two directions

Only horizontal and vertical lines. A framework for every rectangle.

Three primary colours

Red, blue and yellow. Distinct blocks of colour, held in balance.

The non-colours

White, grey and black. Space and structure have their own role.

Why it matters / The legacy

02

Balance doesn’t
need symmetry.

A large red square commands the top right. A small blue square anchors the bottom left; a yellow rectangle marks the bottom right. Thick black lines divide a white ground. This asymmetric balance of just a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

Look closer

A small vocabulary.
A lasting influence.

Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow

Also listed as Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow.

Painted in 1930 by Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944), this oil on canvas in the Kunsthaus Zurich distils De Stijl and Neoplasticism into thick black horizontal and vertical lines balancing primary red, blue and yellow on a white ground.

Painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944)
Year & Medium 1930, oil on canvas
Movement De Stijl (founded 1917)

The picture: a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

De Stijl (1917)

The work belongs to De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Dutch painter Piet Mondrian (1872-1944) painted Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow in 1930 in oil on canvas; it is held in the collection of Kunsthaus Zurich.

Principles of Neoplasticism

Neoplasticism permits only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Why the Work Matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

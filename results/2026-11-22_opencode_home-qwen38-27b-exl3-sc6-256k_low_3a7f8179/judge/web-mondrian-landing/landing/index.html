Piet Mondrian was a Dutch painter. In 1930 he painted Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow in oil on canvas, now in the collection of the Kunsthaus Zurich.

The work belongs to De Stijl, the movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. Its principles, called Neoplasticism, allow only horizontal and vertical lines, the three primary colours red, blue and yellow, and the non-colours white, grey and black.

The picture places a large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right, divided by thick black lines on a white ground.

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture.

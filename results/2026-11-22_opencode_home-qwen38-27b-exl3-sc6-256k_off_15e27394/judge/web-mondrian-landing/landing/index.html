A large red square at the top right, a small blue square at the bottom left and a small yellow rectangle at the bottom right — divided by thick black lines on a white ground. A single canvas that has shaped how we lay out colour for a century.

The movement:
De Stijl

Mondrian painted within De Stijl, the Dutch art movement founded in the Netherlands in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian. The group sought a new visual order built from the most basic elements — straight lines and primary colour — free from decoration and narrative.

Why it matters

Its asymmetric balance of a few primary-colour blocks became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. The composition is a proof that a few shapes, placed with care, can carry an entire design language.

Asymmetric balance

One large block and two small ones, offset — no symmetry, yet the picture feels settled.

A reference for design

Designers and architects have taken the block-and-line layout as a model for composition.

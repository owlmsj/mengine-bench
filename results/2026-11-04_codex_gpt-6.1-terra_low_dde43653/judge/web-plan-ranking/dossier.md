**Finding:** Real wins decisively under the supplied rubric. Collor I and Collor II follow, but their placement is a result of this particular formula, not proof that they fulfilled all their policy promises. The comparison uses calendar-year inflation and the interval to the next listed plan; it does not isolate causation, measure distributional costs, or establish when a plan actually stopped working.

**Method and assumptions.** IPCA values are IBGE's annual accumulated rates for the calendar years immediately before and after launch. A completed month means an anniversary of the launch day has passed by the next launch; partial months are discarded. Month links identify the next launch, while the launch links identify the starting date. *Real's duration is the rubric's fixed assumption, not an observed end date.* Scores are derived from those two source sets using the supplied formula: 70% weight on the proportional drop, bounded at zero and one, and 30% on duration capped at 60 months. Table scores are rounded only for display; ranking uses unrounded calculations. The Real was a multistage process; the currency's introduction is used as its launch, consistent with the rubric's launch year and the [central bank's account](https://www.bcb.gov.br/controleinflacao/planoreal?modalAberto=30anosreal_noticia4). The same date choice sets Collor II's endpoint.

**Editorial caveat:** Collor I's comparison includes the year of Collor II, and Collor II's interval includes other policy changes. The low ranks of Cruzado, Bresser and Verão reflect the rubric's zero floor when subsequent annual inflation exceeds the baseline, even if inflation briefly fell. The central bank describes Real as a staged stabilization process; its account and the IBGE series support the headline, while the formula and date assumptions explain the ordering.

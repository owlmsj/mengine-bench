01 / THE MOVEMENT

A new language.
A universal order.

In 1917, Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian founded De Stijl in the Netherlands. Its vision found expression in a radical visual language: horizontal and vertical lines, primary colours and the non-colours white, grey and black.

Mondrian’s approach, known as Neoplasticism, brought these elements into an asymmetric balance. In this 1930 painting, every block of colour and every stretch of white becomes part of that relationship.

Only the essential lines.

Horizontal and vertical. Thick black divisions create the structure, giving each rectangle its place.

Three primary colours.

Red, blue and yellow. A large red square anchors the top right; smaller blue and yellow accents balance the lower corners.

Space has a voice.

White, grey and black complete the movement’s palette. Here, the white ground is an active part of the composition.

1930 / NOW
02 / THE LEGACY

Few elements.
Lasting influence.

The painting’s asymmetric balance became a reference for modern graphic design and architecture. Its lasting lesson is a powerful one: a few carefully placed elements can shape an entire visual world.
